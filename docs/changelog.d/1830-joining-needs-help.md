- **Meeting bot: `needs_help` is accepted while joining (TC-1830).** A Google Meet bot that needs human help before admission now reports `needs_help` from the `joining` state, instead of only after it reaches `awaiting_admission`. Joining-state escalations are held until 240 seconds after join start and are dropped if the bot has already moved on. The `needs_help` event carries `escalation_reason`, one of `modal_dialog`, `consent_text`, `notes_text`, `waiting_room_indicator`, `no_positive_evidence`, or `unknown`. Poll-path reasons (`waiting_room_timeout_approaching`, `unknown_blocking_state`, `join_error_page_alive`) are unchanged. An escalation the host refuses or fails to receive is not marked sent; it is retried no sooner than 30 seconds later, so a persistent refusal produces at most one request per 30 seconds. A `needs_help` callback the host rejects as malformed (400 or 422) is not retried beyond that cooldown; every other lifecycle callback failure is retried as before. The meeting API accepts `joining → needs_help`; a meeting that ends `failed` from `needs_help` is attributed to the stage it was entered from (`joining` or `awaiting_admission`), including after the API restarts and rehydrates the meeting from its persisted status trail. A meeting with no persisted entry hop keeps the `awaiting_admission` attribution. This mechanism is proven offline only; a live Google Meet validation is still open.
